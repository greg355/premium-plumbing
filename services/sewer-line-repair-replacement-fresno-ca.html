What sewer line repair and replacement involves

A sewer line is the main drain pipe that carries wastewater from your house to the city main or septic tank. When it cracks, sags, corrodes, or fills with tree roots, everything downstream of it starts to fail. Repair or replacement starts with a real diagnosis - not a guess. We run a waterproof sewer camera down the cleanout to see exactly what's happening: root intrusion, offset joints, bellies in the line, collapsed clay tile, or scale buildup in older cast iron.

From there, the fix depends on what we find. A localized crack or root ball can often be cleared with hydro jetting and a spot repair. A fully failed line usually calls for replacement. We do traditional open-trench replacement when access is straightforward, and trenchless methods - pipe bursting or lining - when we need to protect landscaping, driveways, or concrete slabs. Materials are typically PVC or HDPE, sized to match your existing service. Every job ends with a camera verification so you can see the finished line for yourself.

What sewer line repair typically costs

Sewer work covers a wide range because the jobs themselves vary widely. A single root-cutting and hydro jet visit is at the low end. A spot repair - digging up and replacing a few feet of pipe - is a mid-range job. A full sewer line replacement, especially one that runs under a driveway or crosses a city easement, sits at the top of the range. Estimates depend on the length of the line, its depth, pipe material, soil conditions, permit and inspection requirements, whether trenchless is a fit, and how much surface restoration is involved. What's always included in our quotes: the camera inspection, labor, materials, permits when required, and post-work verification. We don't quote sewer jobs over the phone - the camera has to go in the pipe first. Call for a free on-site estimate and you'll know your options before any work starts.

FAQ

How do I know if my sewer line is the problem and not just a clogged drain?
If multiple fixtures back up at once - toilet, shower, and kitchen sink all draining slowly or gurgling - the blockage is usually in the main sewer line, not a branch drain. Wet spots in the yard, sewage smells outside, or repeated clogs after snaking are also strong indicators. A camera inspection confirms it.

Can you repair a sewer line without digging up my whole yard?
Often, yes. Trenchless pipe bursting pulls a new HDPE line through the path of the old one using just two access pits. It's a good fit when the existing line's route is usable and depth allows. We'll tell you honestly whether trenchless makes sense for your property after we camera the line.

Are tree roots really that common in Central Valley sewer lines?
Very common, especially in Fresno neighborhoods with older clay or Orangeburg pipe and mature trees. Roots find the smallest joint gap, grow into the moisture, and expand until they crack the pipe. Hydro jetting clears them; a repair or liner keeps them out long-term.

Do I need a permit to replace my sewer line?
For a full replacement in Fresno, yes - and we handle it. Permits protect you: they mean the work is inspected and documented, which matters if you ever sell the home.

Do you work with landlords and property managers?
Yes. A large share of our work is for property managers across the Central Valley. We can bill directly, coordinate with tenants, and provide the camera footage and reports you need for owner documentation.

Is my sewer line the city's responsibility or mine?
In most Central Valley cities, the homeowner owns the lateral from the house to the city main - sometimes including the tap. We can help you figure out where the responsibility line falls before you commit to any work.
